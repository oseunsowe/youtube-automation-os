## Category: Mysteries

This video covers an unsolved or long-debated mystery -- a disappearance, an unexplained event, a cold case. Focus on:

- The central, unresolved question -- state it clearly early on, and keep the narration oriented around it.
- The known facts, presented in the order they'd be discovered, not just a dry list.
- The leading theories, given fairly -- what evidence supports each one, and what it fails to explain.
- Genuine ambiguity where it exists. It's fine, even good, to end without a tidy answer if the case doesn't have one -- state plainly what remains unknown rather than manufacturing a false resolution.

Avoid: presenting a single fringe theory as the likely truth without noting it's disputed; inventing details (names, quotes, timestamps) that aren't part of the actual historical record to make the story feel more complete.
